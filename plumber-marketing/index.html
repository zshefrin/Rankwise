How Metro Vancouver homeowners actually search for a plumber

Plumbing search splits into two very different intents, and they need different content. "Burst pipe," "no hot water," and "emergency plumber near me" are same-day, same-hour searches; whoever shows up in the map pack with a live phone number and recent reviews gets the call, full stop, no comparison shopping. "Water heater replacement," "repipe cost," and "tankless conversion" are considered searches. Homeowners read a few results, check review counts, and often call two or three businesses before booking. A plumbing GBP profile has to win both: fast-loading and phone-forward for the emergency searcher, review-and-photo-rich for the considered one. Most plumbing marketing fails by optimizing for only one of these.

The job mix underneath those two search types matters for what content actually earns clicks. A drain clog or a leaking valve is a low-ticket, high-frequency job: the kind of work that fills a review profile fast if the follow-up ask is consistent. A repipe or a tankless water heater conversion is rarer and higher-ticket, and it's the job type that benefits most from before-and-after photos and a review that names the specific work done. A plumbing business chasing only the emergency searches misses the compounding value of the considered ones, and vice versa.

What the plumber map pack looks like right now

Across the 206 plumbing businesses Google surfaces in Metro Vancouver, the median business carries 75 reviews, and 91% have a website. That's a market where showing up with a bare-bones GBP profile and no review system is a real handicap. Rating spread is tight (4.87 average), so reviews win on volume and recency more than star count. By city, the picture varies: Vancouver's map pack runs the thickest at a 148-review median (16 tracked businesses), Chilliwack and Coquitlam sit around 75–82, and Abbotsford, Langley, and West Vancouver cluster lower, near 52–53. One outlier business anywhere in the region carries a 12,000-review count that skews any "average"; the median is the number to plan against, not that ceiling.

The number that matters for a new or under-marketed plumbing business: 24.9% of swept plumbing keywords across the region currently have a #1 map-pack result with fewer than 30 reviews. That's a real, beatable share of the field, not most of it, but enough that a targeted push on the right city and keyword combination has a genuine shot at displacing the incumbent, not just chasing a fully-saturated field.

What actually moves a plumbing business up the map pack

Category accuracy first: Google Business Profile lets a plumber set primary and additional categories, and "Plumber" alone undersells a business that also does water heaters, drain cleaning, or gas line work, so each relevant category widens the searches a profile can surface for. After that, review velocity is the single biggest lever in a field where the median business sits at 75 reviews. A steady monthly cadence of new reviews, responded to individually, moves both the ranking signal and the click-through decision a homeowner makes standing in front of the map pack. Photos of finished jobs (not stock plumbing photos) and NAP consistency across the directories Google cross-checks (Yelp, HomeStars, Yellowpages.ca, BBB) round out the profile-level work. A license number on a truck means something to a homeowner standing in their kitchen; it means nothing to a search result. What a search result shows instead is review count, recency, and photos, and that's the trust signal that actually travels online, and the one a marketing program can systematically build.

There's also an ownership objection worth naming directly, because plumbing owners hear it constantly from other agencies: a promise to "get you ranked" with no defined starting point and no defined target isn't a plan, it's a hope. The businesses actually moving through this map pack are the ones with a documented before-and-after position tied to specific keywords in specific cities. Anything less makes it impossible to tell whether a monthly fee is doing anything at all.

What a plumbing marketing partner should deliver every month

  • GBP activity — a real posting cadence, new job photos, and a reply on every new review, not a quarterly check-in.
  • Review velocity tracking — new reviews earned this month versus last, not just a running total.
  • NAP consistency audit — name, address, and phone checked across the directories Google trusts, at minimum quarterly.
  • Map Pack rank tracking — position for each targeted keyword and city, checked regularly, reported monthly against the starting baseline agreed on day one.
  • A written monthly summary — what moved, what didn't, and what's changing next month, in plain language.

If a plumbing business isn't getting all five, there's no way to tell whether the fee is producing calls or just producing invoices.
