## The roofer map pack is the most beatable field Rankwise tracks

Across every unregulated local-service category Rankwise measures, roofing has the highest share of searches where the #1 result is genuinely thin. 33.3% of swept roofer keywords in Metro Vancouver currently show a top map-pack result with fewer than 30 reviews, meaning a third of the field is holding its position on a review count a focused three-to-six-month push can realistically overtake. No other trade in the current tracking comes close: plumbing and landscaping sit in the mid-20s, and HVAC, the category Rankwise has the deepest published proof in, sits at just 4.2%, the hardest field of the six. Roofing is the opposite case, a genuinely open door.

The underlying numbers explain why. Across the 157 roofing businesses Google surfaces regionally, the median sits at just 44 reviews and the highest count tracked anywhere is 485, nowhere near the 12,000-review outliers that show up in categories like plumbing and HVAC. In Pitt Meadows specifically, the median is 37 reviews across 16 tracked businesses. This is a category where a real, sustained review program can move a business into contention within a normal marketing timeline, not a multi-year one.

Worth being direct about the other side of this: a field this thin is also easy for a competitor to notice. A roofing company that waits on this doesn't just miss a window; every review a rival earns while a profile sits idle narrows the same 33.3% opening this page is describing. Openness in a map pack isn't a permanent condition. It's a snapshot of who's shown up consistently and who hasn't yet.

## Why roofing marketing runs on photos and storms, not phone volume

Roofing is a project business, not a call-volume business. A homeowner doesn't search "roofer near me" the way they'd search "emergency plumber." A roof search usually starts after a leak, an inspection, or a storm, and it turns into a five-figure decision that gets researched, not impulse-booked. That changes what wins the click: before-and-after photos of finished roofs, drone or ladder-shot detail that a homeowner can't get from a stock image, and reviews that mention the actual job (a full tear-off, a specific shingle brand, an insurance claim handled cleanly) do more work than posting frequency alone. BC's wet-season pattern also means search demand isn't flat across the year the way some trades' is, so a roofing GBP profile that goes quiet outside storm season is leaving searches on the table when homeowners are actively looking after a leak.

## What actually moves a roofing business up the map pack

With review counts this low across the board, velocity is the highest-leverage lever available. A roofing business that adds real reviews steadily, month over month, is competing directly against a field where the median incumbent has only 44 to defend. Category and service-area accuracy matter too: a roofer that also does gutters, skylights, or commercial flat roofs should have every relevant service entry filled in, since each one is a separate set of searches. Photos carry more weight in roofing than in almost any other trade Rankwise tracks. A profile with real, recent job photos reads as active and trustworthy in a way a text-only listing doesn't, especially for a purchase this size. None of this involves claiming a business is licensed or bonded in the marketing copy itself; that's a claim for the business's own paperwork and its Google-verified profile, not something a search listing should assert.

Insurance-claim work deserves its own mention, because it's a category-specific trust anchor most trades don't have. A homeowner who's just been through a claim process is unusually attentive to reviews that mention a roofer handling the paperwork and adjuster conversation cleanly, not just the tear-off itself. A review program that prompts for that detail, not a generic star rating, captures a signal that's specific to how roofing actually gets sold.
