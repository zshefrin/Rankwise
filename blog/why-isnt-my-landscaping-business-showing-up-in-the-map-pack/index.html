    Why Isn't My Landscaping Business Showing Up in the Map Pack?

    The most common reason a landscaping business doesn't appear in the Map Pack is a mismatch between the Google Business Profile and what Google expects to see for local relevance — primary category, recent activity, and review count. A Rankwise Lab study of 216 landscaping searches across 18 Metro Vancouver cities (July 2026) found 12 of those searches returned no Map Pack result at all — not because nobody in that city does the work, but because Google hasn't resolved a confident local answer yet. That's an opening, not a dead end, and it's worth checking whether your city and service are one of them before you assume you're just being outranked.

    Quick diagnostic — check these in order

    Work through this before changing anything. Most invisibility problems trace back to one or two issues.

    1. Verify the profile is live, not suspended. Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard and look for "Needs attention" alerts. 2. Check for duplicate listings. Search Google Maps for your exact business name. Two entries split your review and proximity signals. 3. Confirm primary category matches your core service. "General contractor" or "Lawn care service" when most of your revenue is design-build landscaping means you're invisible for the searches that actually pay. 4. Look at your last review date. Past 60 days, freshness starts working against you. 5. Check your last GBP post date. Past 30 days, the profile reads as inactive. 6. Verify NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistency across your website, GBP, and directory listings — including trade-association directories if you carry one (e.g. BCLNA for BC landscapers). 7. Count your photos. Fewer than 10, especially none from real job sites, signals low engagement.

    If you're passing all seven, the issue is likely which city and service you're competing in. Read on.

    How many Google reviews does a landscaping business actually need?

    Less than you'd think, and the bar depends on the city. Across the 604 tracked landscaper Map Pack appearances in the Rankwise Lab study, the median review count was 41 — noticeably lower than the roofer study run the day before, where the median was 65. Review count is not a hard gate here the way it looks in more mature categories: 117 of 604 appearances, nearly 1 in 5, belonged to businesses with fewer than 20 reviews.

    Where you compete changes what "enough" means. In West Vancouver, the top competitor alone holds 48% of the city's tracked appearances; in Coquitlam it's 44%. Compare that to Vancouver and Surrey, the two biggest markets by population, where the top competitor holds just 12% and 13% — nobody has locked those up. A business in West Vancouver is fighting an entrenched name. A business in Vancouver proper is fighting fragmentation. Check your own city's numbers before assuming either applies to you.

    Which landscaping services are hardest for Google to answer right now?

    Two service types stood out as the least reliably resolved by Google in the Lab study: irrigation installation returned no Map Pack in 4 of 18 tracked cities (Maple Ridge, New Westminster, Port Moody, Richmond), and tree removal did the same in 4 different cities (Chilliwack, Richmond, West Vancouver, White Rock). If you do that specialty work in one of those cities, that's real ground nobody has claimed yet.

    White Rock stands out on its own: a third of the 12 service categories checked there came back with no Map Pack result at all. That's either an underserved market or one where local landscaping businesses haven't optimized their Google presence for those specific searches — either way, it isn't fully spoken for.

    Does posting on my Google Business Profile actually help me rank?

    Yes, as part of the broader freshness signal Google reads. It won't move rankings the way category or review count does, but a profile that hasn't posted since last fall looks dormant next to one posting weekly.

    What's worth posting:

    • A finished-job photo with a real caption — "Retaining wall and drought-tolerant bed install, Port Coquitlam, this week."
    • A direct answer to a question homeowners actually ask — "How often does a Metro Vancouver lawn actually need aeration?"
    • A specific service-area callout — Coquitlam, Burnaby, Richmond, not just "Metro Vancouver."

    Generic promotional posts read as filler to both Google and the homeowner scrolling past them. A real photo from a real job site doesn't.

    What if my profile looks complete and I'm still not showing up?

    The Map Pack ranks on three factors: relevance (does your profile match the query), distance (how close you are to the searcher), and prominence (how active and authoritative Google judges your business to be). If category, NAP, reviews, and posting cadence all check out, prominence is the remaining lever — and it's the slow one. It builds over 3–6 months through consistent review velocity, directory and association links back to your site, and engagement on your GBP posts (clicks, calls, direction requests).

    There's no single fix for prominence. It compounds when everything else is already right.

    The bottom line

    Category, NAP, and reviews are the fastest wins, in that order — and in a fair share of Metro Vancouver's landscaping categories and cities, the Map Pack itself hasn't fully resolved yet. Work through the diagnostic checklist above before spending on ads or a rebuild.
