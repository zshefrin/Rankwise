## How pet owners actually search

Veterinary search runs on real anxiety in a way few other categories do: "emergency vet open now," "vet near me my dog is bleeding," "24 hour animal hospital [city]." These are searches where the answer to "are you open right now" decides the click before anything else does. Alongside that sits routine, scheduled search, vaccinations, checkups, dental cleanings, and a third, slower pattern: a new pet owner searching for a regular vet for the first time, comparing two or three clinics' profiles the way a new dental patient would. A clinic's Google Business Profile has to answer the urgent question (are you open, do you take emergencies) and the considered one (do you take new patients, what species do you see) for two very different searchers on the same page.

A general practice clinic and a 24-hour or emergency-only animal hospital are genuinely different businesses in how they should show up, even though a pet owner in a panic might search the same phrase for either. A general practice competing on "emergency vet" traffic it can't actually serve after hours risks a bad first call for a client it will never see again for the routine work it's actually built for; getting the profile's categories and hours honestly specific to what a clinic offers matters more here than in most categories.

## What the College of Veterinarians of BC's rules mean for your marketing

CVBC's bylaws bar using a testimonial or endorsement about a treatment's effectiveness for marketing purposes, and the college's separate advertising practice standard goes further, stating ads should contain no testimonials at all. Where it gets genuinely unclear: CVBC's own public guidance treats organic Google and social reviews as generally fine, but says nothing about a marketing agency running a structured review-request campaign on a clinic's behalf. That's a real, unresolved question, not a settled one, and the safe read, until the college clarifies it, is to treat it the same way as chiropractic's explicit ban: no solicited review campaigns for veterinary clients.

The same rules bar guaranteeing a cure or a treatment's effectiveness, bar comparative or superiority claims about a clinic's fees, services, or qualifications, and bar the word "specialist" without the actual specialty registration behind it. What Rankwise runs instead: full GBP build-out, citation and NAP consistency, and content answering real questions (species treated, emergency hours, whether you're accepting new patients) with no cure guarantee, comparison, or unearned specialist claim attached.

## What actually moves a clinic up the map pack

Given how even this field's reviews already run, a 238-review median is a genuinely competitive baseline, profile completeness and content depth carry more of the differentiation than review count alone. A profile with current hours, the right species and service categories, active photos, and an answered Q&A outranks a thinner one even at a similar review count. Citation and NAP consistency across directories matters the same way it does everywhere. Content separating emergency information from routine-care information, rather than one undifferentiated "our services" page, serves both search patterns described above and tends to perform better for exactly that reason.
