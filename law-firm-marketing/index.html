Law firm marketing in Metro Vancouver

Real Google map-pack data, honest costs, and what actually moves position — no client-results claims, because the numbers here are market data, not testimonials.

Yes, a Metro Vancouver law firm should invest in local-search marketing, and it should cost $750 to $1,250 a month for a program built around Google Business Profile, citations, and content, not review-solicitation tactics that work for a roofer but sit in restricted territory for a regulated profession. The Law Society of British Columbia governs how a BC lawyer may market, and that constraint shapes this page more than pricing does.

What the Law Society of BC actually allows

Marketing has to be accurate and can't be misleading, confusing, or deceptive; that's the operating rule under the Code of Professional Conduct's marketing chapter, and it's the line every tactic below has to clear. Firms can advertise practice areas and experience, but can't call themselves a "specialist" in a field unless the Law Society has certified that designation. Fee advertising is allowed but has to be precise about what's included, and a firm has to honour an advertised fee once it's published. None of this bans local-search marketing. GBP optimization, citation consistency, and content answering real client questions all sit comfortably inside the rules. What it does rule out is the review-velocity push that moves categories like roofing or auto repair fastest, because aggressively soliciting reviews framed as case-outcome endorsements sits in the same territory the Code restricts around client vulnerability and misleading claims. Rankwise treats review activity for law firms as a lighter-touch, compliant ask rather than a volume campaign, and leans harder on GBP hygiene and content instead.

How BC clients actually search for a lawyer

"Law firm" is the umbrella label, but almost nobody searches that way. They search their specific problem, and that problem is the real competitive category. A person searches "personal injury lawyer Surrey" after a car accident, "family lawyer Burnaby" mid-divorce, "wills and estate lawyer Coquitlam" after a parent's death, or "immigration lawyer Vancouver" ahead of an application deadline. That's also why Rankwise's exclusivity rule for this vertical isn't "one law firm per city." It's one firm per city per competitive practice area, so a personal-injury practice and a family-law practice in the same city aren't shielded from each other, because they were never real competitors to begin with. Search intent here is high-stakes and considered: people read reviews, check a firm's website, and often research for days before calling, the opposite of an electrician's same-day emergency search.

What the law firm map pack looks like today

Google currently surfaces 248 law firms across the Metro Vancouver and Fraser Valley cities Rankwise tracks, with a metro median of 51 reviews and 97% of firms already running a website (as of 2026-07-09), the highest website rate of any category benchmarked. That makes sense for a profession where a credible site is close to a baseline expectation rather than a differentiator. The city spread is wide: Vancouver's median firm sits at 125 reviews, while Chilliwack's sits at 31, a genuinely different competitive bar depending on where a firm practices. Across the whole tracked field, 15.0% of law firm keywords currently have a top-ranked result with fewer than 30 reviews (as of 2026-07-30), more open than any other regulated category Rankwise tracks — dentist, chiropractor, veterinary, and med spa all sit under 5.5%. The same 2026-07-30 analysis attributes that gap to the field simply being more fragmented, with fewer dominant multi-location incumbents than the health-and-wellness regulated categories carry.

What actually moves a law firm's position, within the rules

Google Business Profile completeness and citation consistency do most of the work here, because review velocity, the fastest lever in unregulated trades, is deliberately the slowest lever Rankwise pulls for this category. A firm's GBP needs accurate practice areas and consistent NAP across legal directories (Avvo, Lawyer Locator, the BC bar's own listings, Google, Yelp). Content built around the real questions a prospective client types into Google before they're ready to call ("how much does a wills lawyer cost in BC," "what to expect at a first personal injury consultation") should answer them directly, without a result guarantee or an unverifiable superlative claim anywhere on the page, since both are restricted under the Code. Where a roofer's page leans on "here's how many reviews the #1 spot has, and we can beat it," a law firm's page has to make the same local-authority case through content depth and profile accuracy instead.

Frequently asked questions

Can a BC law firm legally do local-search marketing?

Yes. GBP optimization, citations, and content are all compliant under the Law Society's Code of Professional Conduct, as long as claims stay accurate and no result is guaranteed.

Why can't a law firm run the same review-push tactics as a roofer?

Aggressively soliciting reviews framed as case endorsements sits close to territory the Code restricts around misleading claims and client vulnerability, so the approach has to be lighter-touch.

What should a law firm budget for marketing per month?

$450 to $1,250/mo depending on scope. Foundation ($750/mo) covers a full one-city Map Pack program; Growth adds practice-area or city expansion.

Is the Metro Vancouver law firm map pack hard to break into?

Less than most regulated categories. 15.0% of tracked keywords have a top result with under 30 reviews, versus under 5.5% for dentist, chiropractor, veterinary, or med spa.

Does "one firm per city" mean Rankwise won't work with two lawyers in the same city?

It means one firm per competitive practice area per city. A family lawyer and a personal-injury lawyer in the same city were never real competitors, so both can be clients.

Can Rankwise claim a firm is a "specialist"?

No. That designation is restricted to lawyers certified as specialists by the Law Society of BC, and no page Rankwise builds will use the term otherwise.

Does the 90-day guarantee say anything about legal outcomes?

No. It's a written commitment about the Map Pack ranking work delivered, agreed before month one; it never references case results or legal outcomes.
