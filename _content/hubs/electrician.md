# Electrician marketing in Metro Vancouver

Yes, most Metro Vancouver electrical contractors should be running some form of local-search marketing, and it should cost somewhere between $750 and $1,250 a month if it's the kind of work that actually moves a Map Pack position. Below that, you're usually paying for a listing cleanup and hoping. Above it, you're usually paying for services an electrical contractor rarely needs, like paid ads or a full site rebuild.

## How electrical searches split into two different jobs

An electrician's inbound calls come from two searches that behave nothing alike, and a marketing plan that treats them the same wastes half its budget. The first is the panel-sparking, breaker-tripping, power-out-at-9pm search: same-day intent, phone-first, decided in the time it takes to scroll three results down. The second is the planned job — a panel upgrade ahead of a renovation, an EV charger install, a rewire on an older Vancouver Special. That buyer reads reviews, checks a website, and sometimes gets two quotes before calling either one. Both searches land in the same Google Map Pack, but they reward different signals. The emergency searcher trusts whoever answers the phone and looks legitimate fast. The planned-job searcher digs into review volume and photos of finished work before deciding who's trustworthy enough for a panel-upgrade job. A page or a GBP profile built for only one of these leaves calls on the table from the other, and it's the planned-job segment — panel upgrades, EV charger installs — that's grown the most in the Lower Mainland over the past couple of years as older housing stock gets renovated and more households add a second vehicle charger.

## What the electrical map pack actually looks like right now

Google currently surfaces 275 electrical businesses across the cities Rankwise tracks in Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley, with a median of 55 reviews and an average rating of 4.84 (as of 2026-07-20). That median is low compared to a category like auto repair, where the typical business Google surfaces sits north of 170 reviews. Electrical is a field where a business with 60 to 80 reviews is already ahead of half its competition, not buried under an unreachable pile. The spread by city matters more than the metro number: Delta's median electrician has just 34 reviews, while North Vancouver's sits at 72. A contractor licensed to work anywhere in the Lower Mainland should know which of their cities is the easier fight before deciding where to spend a marketing dollar first, because the same review-request habit closes a much wider gap in Delta than it does in North Vancouver. 91% of the businesses Google shows have a website, so a site is table stakes here, not a differentiator. The review count and the ongoing GBP activity are where the real competitive gap sits.

## What actually moves an electrician up the map pack

Review velocity moves this category faster than almost anything else in it, because the typical incumbent's review count is genuinely low and Google weighs recency alongside total volume. A contractor closing 15 to 20 jobs a month who asks for a review after each one can out-accumulate a competitor sitting on an old, static profile within a couple of quarters. Behind that sits a Google Business Profile kept current with real job photos: panel swaps, EV charger installs, service-panel labels, the kind of work photos that read as more credible than stock imagery. Consistent NAP (name, address, phone) across directories keeps Google from second-guessing which listing is real, and city-specific content that answers what people actually type ("panel upgrade cost Burnaby," "EV charger installer Coquitlam") outperforms one generic "electrical services" page trying to rank everywhere at once.

One honest constraint, stated plainly: electrical work in BC runs through a licensing and permitting system. Technical Safety BC governs who can perform electrical work commercially, and homeowners can only self-permit work on their own principal residence, not a strata unit, a duplex, or a home business. Rankwise does not verify or assert any business's licence status on a page, and never implies Rankwise itself holds a trade licence. That's between the contractor and Technical Safety BC, and any homeowner hiring an electrician should confirm it directly rather than take a review count as a proxy for it.

## What a monthly program should actually include

- Weekly Google Business Profile posts and photo updates tied to real completed jobs, not stock content
- An active review-request workflow triggered after each closed job, with responses posted to every review within a few days
- A citation/NAP audit run at least quarterly across directories like Yelp, HomeStars, and trade-specific listings, to catch drift before Google penalizes inconsistency
- City-and-service-specific page content covering panel upgrades, EV chargers, rewiring, and emergency service separately, rather than one page trying to cover everything
- Monthly Map Pack position tracking against named competitors, reported in writing, not just promised
