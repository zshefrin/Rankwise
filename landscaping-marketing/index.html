Landscaping is a recurring-revenue business, and your marketing should track that

A landscaping search covers two very different buyer intents, and they need different content to win. "Landscaper near me" or "lawn care Surrey" in spring is often the start of a maintenance relationship: weekly or biweekly mowing, seasonal cleanup, a customer who books once and stays for years. "Landscape design," "retaining wall," or "backyard renovation" is a considered, higher-ticket project search, researched and compared before booking. A landscaping GBP profile that only shows mow-and-blow work misses the design-and-build searcher, and one that only shows finished hardscape projects misses the far larger volume of maintenance searches. The category rewards profiles that show both.

The economics behind this are what make landscaping unusual among the trades on this hub. A single won maintenance client can generate a booking every one to two weeks for an entire season, sometimes renewing year over year, where a plumbing or roofing win is typically a single job. That changes how a landscaping business should think about acquisition cost: a map-pack position that converts even a modest number of maintenance searches into standing accounts compounds in a way a one-off-project trade's win doesn't.

What the landscaper map pack looks like right now

Landscaping is the most fragmented category Rankwise tracks by business count, with 256 businesses surfacing across Metro Vancouver, more than plumbing, roofing, or HVAC. That also means the median incumbent is thinner: just 37 reviews regionally, the lowest median of any trade in this data. Across eight tracked cities the spread runs from New Westminster's 25-review median (15 businesses) up to Chilliwack's 63 (16 businesses), with Delta (34), Pitt Meadows (36), Surrey (49), Vancouver (48), Langley (51), and Abbotsford (44) filling in between. The regional review ceiling tops out at 416, nothing close to the multi-thousand-review outliers that show up in plumbing or HVAC, which means a genuinely new landscaping profile isn't chasing an impossible incumbent anywhere in the region.

24.0% of swept landscaper keywords currently show a #1 map-pack result with fewer than 30 reviews, a real, sizeable share of searches where the top spot is holding on volume a focused push can catch. Combined with a 256-business field, the practical read is that there's room in nearly every city tracked, and the fight is winnable without needing thousands of reviews to get there.

What actually moves a landscaper up the map pack

With a 37-review metro median, review velocity does the most work. A landscaping business that earns a steady trickle of new reviews through the season, especially timed around spring cleanup and fall cleanup when search volume peaks, is outpacing most of the field just by staying consistent. Service-category completeness matters in a way that's specific to this trade: "Landscaper" alone doesn't capture a business that also does irrigation, hardscaping, or snow removal, and each added category opens a new set of searches. Photos matter differently here than in roofing. A landscaping profile benefits from seasonal, current photos, not one spring photoshoot reused for three years, since Google and searchers both read a stale photo gallery as a stale business. Rounding it out: NAP consistency across the directories Google trusts, and a service-area setting that reflects the cities actually served rather than a single default city.

Timing the ask matters more in landscaping than in most trades on this hub. A review request sent the week a maintenance visit happens lands very differently than one sent months later after the yard looks the same as every other week. The review is strongest right after a visible seasonal change (a spring cleanup, a fall leaf haul, a new bed installed), which is also exactly when a homeowner is most likely to be thinking about the business at all.

FAQ

Is landscaping marketing worth it if most of the value is repeat maintenance clients, not one-off jobs?

That's exactly the case for it. A map-pack win in landscaping tends to convert to a recurring account, not a single invoice, which is a stronger return than most one-off-project trades see from the same spend.

How thin is the landscaper map pack really?

The metro median is 37 reviews across 256 tracked businesses, and 24.0% of swept keywords have a #1 result under 30 reviews. That's thinner than plumbing or HVAC, thicker than roofing.

Which Metro Vancouver city is easiest for a landscaping business to compete in right now?

Does landscaping marketing need to run year-round?

Search demand peaks in spring and fall cleanup seasons, but a profile that goes dark in the off-season loses the consistency signal that GBP and searchers both reward. Steady beats seasonal-only.

Does a landscaping business need a different marketing plan for maintenance versus design-build work?
