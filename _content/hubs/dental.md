# Dental practice marketing in Metro Vancouver

Yes, a Metro Vancouver dental practice benefits from local-search help, and it should cost $750-$1,250/month, not a flat web-design invoice. The catch is that the tactic most local-marketing programs lead with, asking every satisfied patient for a Google review, sits inside advertising rules a trades page never has to think about.

## How patients actually search for a dentist

Dental search splits three ways, and each needs its own page, not one generic "dentist near me" landing spot. New-patient searches lean considered: "dentist accepting new patients [city]," "family dentist near me," "dentist that direct bills [insurer]." These are decisions made over a few days, comparing two or three practices' Google profiles before a call gets made. Emergency search exists but is a smaller share than in trades work: "emergency dentist open now," "broken tooth dentist today." These convert fast, to whichever profile answers "are you open" first. The third pattern is retention, not acquisition. Most people who already have a dentist don't re-search every six months for a cleaning, which is why new-patient acquisition, not recall reminders, is where local search earns its keep.

There's a real seasonal wrinkle worth planning around: most extended-health dental benefits reset on a calendar year, so search volume for "dentist accepting new patients" and similar phrases tends to soften over the December holidays and pick back up in January as people look to use a fresh year's coverage. A profile and content plan that's dormant in that window loses ground it doesn't need to lose.

## What BC's dental advertising rules mean for your marketing

BC's dental regulator, the College of Oral Health Professionals, restricts what a practice can call itself in advertising. Only a certified specialist can advertise using a specialist designation like "orthodontist," regardless of which procedures a general practice performs. Regulated professions generally, dentistry included, also carry testimonial and review-solicitation limits that an unregulated trade doesn't face, so the systematic, templated review-ask campaigns that work for a plumber aren't the safe default for a dental client.

What Rankwise runs instead: a fully built-out Google Business Profile (services, categories, photos, Q&A, hours, the parts of the profile that are compliance-neutral and still drive ranking), citation and NAP consistency across directories, city- and service-specific content that never claims a credential you don't hold, and honest map-pack position tracking so you know whether any of it is working. Reviews you earn organically stay yours. What changes is that we don't run a solicitation campaign to generate more of them.

## What actually moves a dental practice up the map pack

Three things carry most of the weight once the compliance line is drawn. Profile completeness: a profile missing services, hours, or photos loses to one that has them, independent of review count. Citation consistency: the same name, address, and phone number everywhere a search engine or directory can find it. And content that answers what a prospective patient is actually asking, insurance direct-billing, sedation options, whether you're taking new patients, instead of generic "we care about your smile" copy that could sit on any dental site in the country.

## A note on multi-dentist practices

Many Metro Vancouver dental practices run two or more dentists under one clinic name, sometimes each with a distinct specialty focus (general, cosmetic, pediatric) inside the same competitive category. That's a real operational detail an agency needs to manage correctly: the Google Business Profile represents the practice, not any one dentist, so services and photos need to reflect the whole team, while any specialist designation used in content still has to trace back to whichever dentist actually holds that certification, not the practice as a whole.
