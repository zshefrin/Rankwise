    What Primary Category Should an HVAC Contractor Pick on Google Business Profile?

    Pick the most specific category that is genuinely true of the business. Google's published guideline is to choose categories that complete the statement "this business is a" rather than "this business has a", and to use as few of them as possible to describe the core business. For a contractor whose revenue is furnaces and heat pumps that means "Heating Contractor"; for a cooling-led business, "Air Conditioning Contractor"; for one genuinely built on both, "HVAC Contractor" is the accurate answer rather than a weak one.

    The category you pick is the highest-weight GBP signal Google uses to decide which searches your listing is eligible for — Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors survey scored it first out of 187 factors for local pack rankings in 2026, though it's a survey of practitioners rather than anything Google has confirmed. Get the category wrong and the rest of the profile is competing in searches you were never entered into.

We could not reproduce that figure from data we're able to publish, so it has been removed rather than restated more softly. Nothing on this page now depends on it. Where a number would once have gone, you'll find the method for getting the real one for your own city — which was always the more useful answer.

    Which GBP Primary Category Should an HVAC Contractor Actually Use?

    Four are worth considering. Which one is right is a question about your business, not a leaderboard:

    1. Heating Contractor — for contractors whose primary revenue is furnace installation, repair, and heating systems. 2. Air Conditioning Contractor — if cooling (central A/C, ductless mini-splits) is the larger share of your revenue or your strongest seasonal driver. 3. HVAC Contractor — if the business is genuinely split across heating and cooling with no dominant service. It's broader than the two above, which is the correct trade-off when it's the truthful description. 4. Furnace Repair Service — narrow, and worth it only if repair work genuinely is the business rather than one service among several.

    Do not use: "Mechanical Contractor," "Plumber," "General Contractor," or any dual-trade category as your primary. Google reads primary category as your business identity — a plumbing category pulls you into plumbing results, not HVAC results.

    Why Does Primary Category Matter More Than Secondary Categories?

    Google itself doesn't publish a weighting. What it does say is that local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that relevance is "how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for" (Improve your local ranking on Google). The primary category is the most explicit relevance statement your profile makes.

    The closest thing to a public ranking of the factors is Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors survey, where local search practitioners score the factors from experience. In the 2026 edition, primary GBP category came first out of 187 — ahead of review count and proximity. Whitespark is explicit that the respondents have no special access to Google's algorithm, so treat it as the strongest available opinion rather than a measurement.

    Secondary categories expand the services you're associated with, but they don't anchor the primary. If your listing says "Mechanical Contractor," you are not a strong match when someone searches "heating contractor Burnaby."

    The specific names matter too. Google's taxonomy is exact-match. "Heating and Cooling Contractor" is not the same category as "Heating Contractor" — they trigger different query associations. Use the exact Google-recognized category name when you select it in the GBP dashboard.

    What Secondary Categories Should HVAC Contractors Add?

    After setting your primary, add a secondary for each distinct service you actually deliver — and stop there. Google's guidance is to use as few categories as possible to describe the core business, and it notes that choosing a specific category implicitly covers the broader ones above it, so a longer list is not a stronger one. For a Metro Vancouver HVAC contractor, the candidates are:

    • Air Conditioning Contractor
    • Furnace Repair Service
    • Heat Pump Installation Service
    • Heating Contractor (if not primary)
    • Ductwork Installation or Duct Cleaning Service (if you offer it)
    • Boiler Supplier or Boiler Repair Service (if applicable)

    There is a cap on secondaries; the dashboard will tell you when you reach it. Treat it as a limit, not a target. Adding "Plumber" when you don't do plumbing dilutes the signal and puts you in front of the wrong searches. Each secondary should name a service that actually shows up in your booking calls.

    Heat Pump Installation Service is worth a look for Metro Vancouver contractors, because BC Hydro and CleanBC have run heat pump rebate programs for several years and the work has followed the incentives. Before adding it on our say-so, check it: put "heat pump installation" into Google Trends with the region set to British Columbia, and see the shape of the line yourself. If you do the work and the demand is there, the category belongs on your profile.

    Does Changing Your Primary Category Affect Your Current Rankings?

    It can. Category is the field that determines which queries you're matched against, so changing it changes the set of searches you compete in — that's the mechanism, and it's the reason the change registers rather than sitting inert. We're not going to quote you a number of days, because we don't have one we can show you, and the honest answer is that it depends on your market and how far the old category was from the truth.

    The risk runs both directions. Switching from "HVAC Contractor" to "Heating Contractor" may improve how you match heating queries while reducing appearances for generic "HVAC" searches. Whether that's a good trade depends on which queries actually produce your calls — in a lot of Metro Vancouver markets "furnace repair Burnaby" carries more buying intent than "HVAC Burnaby," but that's a question you can answer from your own booking history rather than from a blog post.

    Which is why the real advice is: measure it. Before changing your primary, pull 30 days of GBP Insights so you have a baseline — search appearance count, discovery searches, top queries — and re-check 30 days after. Then you'll know what the change did for your business, which is the only sample size that matters to you.

    How Do You Know Which Category Your Competitors Are Using?

    You can see it without any tools. Search "HVAC contractor [your city]" in Google and open the Map Pack listings of the top 2–3 competitors. Their primary category appears directly under their business name in the listing preview. Write down what the top-3 in your city are using — this is your category benchmark.

    Do it per city, not per region. Metro Vancouver is not one market — the pack in Vancouver and the pack in Maple Ridge are different fields with different names in them — so a regional average would be the wrong input even if it were accurate. Run the search for each city you actually take jobs in.

    If the three businesses beating you are all listed as something you're not, that's the most concrete finding you'll get from an hour of profile work, and it cost you two minutes per city.

    What Else Should HVAC Contractors Know About GBP Category Optimization?

    Category is the field to check first, but it compounds with three other GBP signals:

    • Business description: Use the primary category name naturally in the first sentence — "Bridgeview Heating serves Burnaby and Coquitlam as a heating contractor and air conditioning contractor." The description doesn't rank you on its own, but it reinforces what the category already says.
    • Services section: Add specific services under the Services tab (GBP dashboard → Edit profile → Services). Each service entry is indexed separately. "Furnace installation," "heat pump installation," and "ductwork replacement" are all discrete ranking opportunities.
    • Photo labels and captions: Job photos uploaded with descriptive filenames and captions (e.g., "furnace installation Burnaby — Carrier 96% AFUE") reinforce category relevance to Google's image-indexing systems.

    None of these substitute for the right primary category. They reinforce it.
